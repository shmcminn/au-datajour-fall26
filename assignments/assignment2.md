# Assignment 2: Build onto an existing database

**Due Sept. 23**

## The assignment

Find an existing database that could help answer a reporting question. Download or copy the data into Excel or Google Sheets, then identify an important piece of information that is missing.

Add at least one new field (column) to the database by researching the missing information from another source. Add information for at least **10 records**. Include a column with a source link for every record you add and a `Notes` column for anything that's important to note about a specific record.

The new field must require outside research. Do not add a column that can be fully calculated or derived from information already in the database. For example, if a police database includes a start time and an end time, calculating the length of the incident by subtracting the two would not satisfy this assignment. You need to find information that is not already present in the original data.

Your new dataset may be created in Excel or Google Sheets. Use Markdown to link to it in your `assignment2.md` file. This can be done by uploading the spreadsheet to Github or by linking to a Google Sheet. If you use Google Sheets, make sure the sheet is shared to be viewable by anyone with the link.

## Your written submission

In `assignment2.md`, include:

1. A link to the original database.
2. A description of who created the original database, what it covers and any important limitations. Consider its date range, geographic scope, missing information, definitions and collection methods.
3. The reporting question you could answer after adding the new information and why the missing information is newsworthy or useful to your audience.
6. A link to your expanded dataset.
7. A brief note about any difficult judgment calls, conflicting information or unusual records. You may also record these details in the optional `Notes` field in your dataset.
1. An AI disclosure


## Examples (don't use these - just for your inspiration!)

### Entertainment

Download a list of the top 40 songs from a music chart. Add the genre of each song using a reliable music-industry, artist or record-label source. You could then ask: **Which genres are most represented among the top songs, and are some genres more likely to reach the top of the chart?**

### Sports

Download a database of the salaries or earnings of top athletes. Add each athlete’s country of birth using an official team biography, league profile or other reliable source. You could then ask: **Are athletes from certain countries overrepresented among the highest earners?**

### Environment

Download a database of extreme weather events in a city or region. Add the number of fatalities associated with each event by reviewing news reports, government releases or official records. You could then ask: **Which types of extreme weather events have been deadliest, and has that changed over time?**

Students should explain how they handled reports that gave different fatality counts, as well as deaths that were indirect or occurred after the event.

## AI usage

You may also use AI to brainstorm missing fields, extract possible information from public documents or check your completed dataset for accuracy. You may not treat AI-generated information as a source. Verify every added record with the linked source and disclose any AI use in your written submission.
