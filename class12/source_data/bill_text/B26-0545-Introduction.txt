Dear Secretary Howard, 
Today, with Councilmembers Janeese Lewis George and Trayon White, I am introducing the 
“River East at Grandview Homeownership Relief and Restoration Amendment Act of 2025.” This 
legislation will help restore dignity, opportunity, and stability to families who have endured years 
of displacement and uncertainty. 
Between 2017 and 2019, 46 first-time homebuyers, many of them Black residents, purchased 
homes at River East at Grandview in Ward 8. These homes represented more than shelter; they 
were the promise of equity, stability, and generational wealth. However, structural issues forced 
families to evacuate in 2021. Since then, many have remained in limbo, unable to afford new 
homes, living with their belongings in storage, and struggling to rebuild what was lost. 
This bill acknowledges the emotional and financial toll that displacement has had on these families 
and takes meaningful steps to help them move forward. It recognizes that homeownership is not 
just a transaction; it is a foundation for life, and when that foundation is shaken, the consequences 
ripple for years. 
The legislation provides targeted relief through existing District programs to former River East 
homeowners, tailored to their current housing status. It offers HPAP grants to those who have not 
yet repurchased, converts HPAP loans to grants for those who have, and adjusts Inclusionary 
Zoning affordability covenants to help owners access the equity they would have built in their 
Talbert Street homes. It also ensures that relief is not taxed as income and requires annual reporting 
to track progress and challenges. These are actionable measures the District can implement using 
existing, budgeted programs. 
This bill is designed to help families reclaim the stability and opportunity they once had and still 
deserve. We urge our colleagues to support this legislation and stand with the residents of River 
East as they continue their journey toward healing and home. 
Please contact the Committee on Housing staff at housing@dccouncil.gov with any questions.

amended as follows: 31 
 (a) Section 2132 (D.C. Official Code § 42–2081) is amended as follows:  32 
  (1) Paragraph (l) is amended by striking the period and inserting the phrase 33 
“between July 2017 and March 2019.” in its place. 34

(2) A new paragraph (m) is added to read as follows: 35 
 “(m) ‘Former Property Owner’ means an individual who purchased a condominium unit 36 
at the River East at Grandview Condominium between July 2017 and March 2019 and who no 37 
longer owns that unit.” 38 
 (b) A new Section 2135 is added to read as follows: 39 
“Sec. 2135 HPAP loan conversion to grant and shortening the length of IZ affordability 40 
covenant for Property Owners and Former Property Owners 41 
“(a) The Department of Housing and Community Development (‘DHCD’), in 42 
coordination with the District of Columbia Housing Finance Agency (‘DCHFA’), shall provide 43 
relief under this section to individuals defined as Property Owners or Former Property Owners  44 
based on their homeownership status, as follows:  45 
(1) A property owner or former property owner who has not purchased another 46 
primary residence since leaving the Property shall be eligible for assistance through the Home 47 
Purchase Assistance Program (‘HPAP’), established under the Home Purchase Assistance Fund 48 
Act of 1978, effective September 20, 1978 (D.C. Law 2-135; D.C. Official Code § 42–2601 et 49 
seq.), in the form of a grant, toward the purchase of a primary residence in the District of 50 
Columbia. The applicant shall be excluded from credit requirements for the HPAP program.  51 
(2) Property owners or former property owners who previously received HPAP 52 
assistance to purchase a primary residence since leaving the Property in the District of Columbia 53 
shall have their outstanding HPAP loan converted to a grant. This provision shall not apply to 54 
any subsequent purchase beyond the first purchased primary residence. 55 
(A) DHCD and The Office of Tax and Revenue shall record any release, 56 
modification, or other instrument necessary to reflect the conversion in the land records. 57

(3) Property Owners or Former Property Owners who purchased or will purchase 58 
as their next primary residence following leaving the Property an IZ unit pursuant to 14 DCMR § 59 
2200 et seq. shall be given prioritization when applying for a IZ ownership unit and have any 60 
affordability covenant recorded on that unit terminate 15 years after the date that the Property 61 
Owner or Former Property Owner closed on a condominium unit at the Property. This provision 62 
shall not apply to any subsequent purchase beyond the first qualifying unit. 63 
(A) DHCD shall ensure that the termination of a covenant under this 64 
paragraph is recorded and publicly available.  65 
“(b) Notwithstanding any other provision of law:  66 
  (1) Any amount received under this section shall be excluded from District gross 67
