Statement of Introduction  
Institution of Higher Education Sexual Misconduct Reporting and  
Resource Accessibility Act of 2025 
January 6, 2025 
 
Today, I am introducing the Institution of Higher Education Sexual Misconduct Reporting and 
Resource Accessibility Act of 2025, along with Councilmembers Janeese Lewis George, Robert 
C. White, Jr., Charles Allen, Matthew Frumin, Brooke Pinto, and Brianne K. Nadeau. This 
measure was first introduced in Council Period 25.  
 
Students attending college in the District should be certain of accruing benefits such as job 
security, exceptional networking opportunities, high earnings, and personal development, but for 
some students, experiencing sexual misconduct during their educational career is also highly 
likely.  
 
Sexual misconduct is a prevalent crime against women, transgender people, genderqueer people, 
and nonbinary people at institutions of higher education. According to the American Association 
of Universities’ 2020 campus climate survey on sexual assault and sexual misconduct, 
nationally, 59% of undergraduate women reported experiencing harassing behavior since 
enrolling at school, and 23% of transgender, genderqueer, and nonbinary students experienced 
unwanted sexual contact.1 Less than half of students surveyed who had experienced sexual 
misconduct thought it likely that an official at a school would take an allegation of sexual 
misconduct seriously. Only 29.5% of victims contacted at least one program or resource to 
support them, but only 20.6% of these contacted Title IX and 17.9% contacted their campus 
victim services office. Institutions of higher education are required to publicly report reported 
incidents of sexual violence, but low survivor reporting leads to low institutional reporting. Only 
one third of students felt they were very or extremely knowledgeable about the definition of 
sexual assault, where to get help, and how to report it, which underscores the importance of 
training so students understand what sexual assault is and how to get help. It is important that a 
student survivor feel confident in the resources and processes that are available to them through 
their school during one of the worst times of their life.  
 
While the District continues to work toward the goal of preventing sexual assault and providing 
quality resources for survivors, it is equally important to sharpen the tools at our disposal that

This legislation would further the District’s commitment to supporting survivors of sexual 
misconduct by:  
§ Requiring Institutions of higher education in the District to retain a confidential resource 
advisor (CRA) to provide emergency and ongoing support to reporting parties at 
institutions of higher education. 
o The CRA would be required to be experienced in sexual misconduct work, 
disseminate information to reporting parties and to institution staff and students.  
o A CRA would provide victims with assistance regarding navigating processes in 
the institution, in law enforcement, being connected to resources following an 
experience of sexual misconduct, and other supports the victim requests.  
o All communications between the reporting party and the CRA would be 
confidential, and the reporting party would have to approve the CRA 
communicating with outside entities in most circumstances. 
o This section of the bill also creates flexibility for small institutions by allowing 
them to partner with another institution or enter into a Memorandum of 
Understanding (MOU) with a local victim advocacy organization to provide these 
services. 
o In the District, George Washington University, American University, and Howard 
University currently have CRAs. 
§ Requiring amnesty protections for reporting parties of sexual misconduct for violations of 
an institution’s student conduct policy if the violation occurred immediately before, 
during, or immediately after the incident of sexual misconduct, unless the violation put at 
immediate and severe risk the health and safety of others.   
§ Requiring institutions to facilitate mandatory annual sexual misconduct trainings for all 
students with information about consent, the impact of drugs and alcohol on an 
individual’s ability to consent, procedures following sexual misconduct, contact 
information for the CRA, bystander intervention strategies, the rights of reporting parties, 
and the institution’s responsibilities regarding no-contact orders, orders of protection, or 
restraining orders. 
§ Requiring institution of higher education personnel who are involved in an institution of 
higher education's investigative and disciplinary process regarding sexual misconduct to 
receive annual training in handling sexual misconduct complaints and in the operations of 
the institution's disciplinary process. 
§ Requiring that CRAs, Title IX coordinators, and an institution of higher education’s 
public safety personnel be educated in trauma-informed responses, and investigators to 
receive annual training on conducting investigations using trauma-informed responses.  
§ Establishing minimum criteria for institutions’ sexual misconduct policies.  
§ Allowing institutions to grant students who report experiencing sexual misconduct to 
specific institution personnel a waiver from minimum GPA, credit hour load, or other 
academic requirements or disciplinary record standards if the reporting student consents

Sexual misconduct is uprooted by safe, trustworthy, and transparent environments where 
institutions’ sexual misconduct policies are informed by recent and relevant data, where 
reporting parties can access information and resources from a confidential source present solely 
to provide support, where the privacy of reporting parties is respected, and where staff are 
regularly trained to respond to sexual misconduct. The Council is committed to ensuring safe 
educational environments for students and personnel at the District’s institutions of higher 
education and to ensuring that our institutions empower survivors to seek resources following 
any incident of sexual misconduct, and I look forward to working with my colleagues to realize 
these goals.

Sec. 2. Definitions. 49 
For the purposes of this act, the term: 50 
(1) “Institution of higher education” or “institution” shall have the same meaning 51 
as provided in section 101 of the Higher Education Act (20 U.S.C. §1001).  52 
(2) “Reporting party” means a student or employee of an institution who reports 53 
having experienced an incident of sexual misconduct to the institution. 54 
(3) “Responding party” means a student, employee, or other individual who has 55 
been accused of an incident of sexual misconduct by a reporting party. 56 
(4) “Student” means an individual who is enrolled at least part-time in a credit-57 
bearing program through a public or private degree-granting institution of higher education.  58 
(5) “Sexual misconduct” shall have the same meaning as the following terms: 59 
(A) Dating violence, domestic violence, and stalking, as defined in the 60 
Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2022 (Pub. L. 117-103, 136 Stat. 49);  61 
(B) Sexual assault, as defined in the Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus 62 
Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act (20 USC § 1092); or 63 
(C) Sexual harassment, as defined in Title IX of the Education 64 
Amendments of 1972 (34 CFR 106.30(a)).  65 
(6) “Title IX coordinator” means an employee designated by an institution whose 66 
responsibilities include those required by 34 C.F.R. § 106.8(a). 67

(7) “Trauma-informed response" means a response involving an understanding of 68 
the complexities of sexual misconduct through training centered on the neurobiological impact of 69 
trauma, the influence of myths and stereotypes surrounding the causes and impacts of trauma, 70 
perpetration methodology, and methods to conduct investigations. 71

Sec. 4. Amnesty protections for reporting parties. 201

9 
(a) A reporting party or a witness to an incident of sexual misconduct who reports an 202 
incident of sexual misconduct to an institution or local or federal law enforcement shall not be 203 
subject to a disciplinary proceeding or sanction for a violation of the institution of higher 204 
education's student conduct policy that occurred immediately preceding, during, or immediately 205 
following the incident of sexual misconduct unless the institution determines that the report was 206 
not made in good faith or that the violation put at immediate and severe risk the health and safety 207 
of other individuals.  208 
(b) Notwithstanding an institution’s code of conduct, an institution shall not take 209 
disciplinary action against reporting parties for consensual sexual activity related to the incident 210 
or other consensual sexual activity revealed during an investigation into the reported incident.  211 
Sec. 5. Annual sexual misconduct awareness training.  212 
(a) An institution of higher education shall provide mandatory annual sexual misconduct 213 
primary prevention and awareness programming that is trauma-informed and gender-inclusive 214 
for all students of the institution. The training shall be mandatory and shall include: 215 
(1) An explanation of consent in reference to sexual activity and sexual 216 
relationships; 217 
(2) The role that drugs and alcohol play in an individual's ability to consent; 218 
(3) Procedures victims should follow if sexual misconduct has occurred, including 219 
information in writing about: 220 
(i) The importance of preserving evidence to document sexual 221 
misconduct; 222 
(ii) Procedures to report an incident of sexual misconduct, including 223 
confidential and anonymous disclosure; and 224

10 
(iii) Information on the institution's procedures for resolving sexual 225 
misconduct complaints and the range of sanctions or penalties the institution may impose on 226 
students and employees found responsible for a violation; 227 
(4) The name, contact information, and role of the confidential resource advisor; 228 
(5) Safe and positive strategies for bystander intervention that may be carried out 229 
by an individual to prevent harm or reduce risk; 230 
(6) The rights of reporting parties and the institution’s responsibilities regarding 231 
orders of protection, no contact orders, restraining orders, or similar lawful orders issued by a 232 
criminal or civil court; and 233 
(7) Opportunities for ongoing sexual misconduct prevention and sexual 234 
misconduct awareness training and programming. 235 
(b) Trainings shall be culturally responsive and reflect students’ experiences, including 236 
race, color, ethnicity, national origin, religion, economic status, disability, sexual orientation, 237 
gender identity, and reproductive status. 238

(a) An individual who participates in the implementation of an institution of higher 240 
education's investigative and disciplinary process, including an individual responsible for 241 
resolving complaints of reported incidents, an individual responsible for conducting a meeting, 242 
hearing or other disciplinary proceeding or informal resolution process, and the confidential 243 
resource advisor employed by the institution shall have annual training in handling sexual 244 
misconduct complaints and in the operations of the institution's disciplinary process, which shall 245 
include: 246

(2) Information about types of conduct that constitute sexual misconduct; 249 
(3) Information about consent and the role drugs and alcohol may play in an 250

(3) The effects of trauma, including the manifestation of neurobiological and 252

(4) Cultural competence training regarding the impacts of sexual misconduct 254

(5) Techniques to communicate sensitively and compassionately with a reporting 258

(6) Techniques to communicate sensitively with a responding party, including an 260

(7) Training and information regarding how sexual misconduct may impact 262

Sec. 7. Institution personnel trauma-informed training requirements.   264 
(a) Each institution of higher education shall ensure that its confidential resource advisor, 265

trauma-informed responses. 267

Sec. 8. Minimum criteria for institutional sexual misconduct policies. 271 
(a) Institutions of higher education in the District of Columbia shall ensure that the 272 
institution’s sexual misconduct policy includes: 273 
(1) Information on where victims of sexual assault can receive immediate 274 
emergency assistance following an alleged incident of sexual misconduct, which shall include: 275 
(A) The name and location of medical facilities where an individual may 276 
request that a medical forensic exam be administered by a trained sexual assault forensic health 277 
care provider; 278 
(B) Descriptions and contact information for local rape crisis centers and 279 
domestic violence resource providers, and a description of the services provided by such centers;  280 
(C) The telephone number and website for a national 24-hour sexual 281 
assault hotline; 282 
(D) Information on any programs that may provide financial assistance to 283 
a student with the cost of emergency medical assistance or related travel; and 284 
(E) Information regarding the legal and institutional obligations of the 285 
Title IX coordinator and employees of the Title IX office; and 286 
(2) Descriptions of the roles and responsibilities and contact information for the 287 
confidential resource advisor and the institution's Title IX coordinator. 288

Sec. 9. Waivers for student reporting parties. 292 
(a) Institutions may grant to a student who has reported an experience of sexual assault to 293 
the institution’s confidential resource advisor, Title IX coordinator, public safety personnel, or 294 
local law enforcement and has given written consent to those individuals to confirm to the 295 
institution the incident of sexual misconduct, a waiver from requirements that the student 296 
maintain a minimum grade point average, credit hour load, or satisfy other academic 297 
requirements or disciplinary record standards in order to maintain eligibility for institutional 298 
scholarships and grants; provided, that academic achievement standards required to maintain 299 
eligibility for federal student financial aid programs shall not be waived.  300 
(b) The institution and its employees shall maintain confidentiality related to a waiver 301 
and related personal identifying information unless disclosure is required specifically for the 302 
purposes of executing the waiver. 303

Sec. 11. Enforcement. 335 
(a) Upon determination, after reasonable notice and opportunity for a hearing, that an 336 
institution of higher education has violated or failed to carry out any provision of this act or any 337 
rule adopted under this act, the Higher Education Licensure Commission may impose a civil fine 338 
upon an institution for each violation not to exceed $59,000, or one percent of an institution’s 339 
annual operating budget, whichever is lower. The Higher Education Licensure Commission shall 340 
use revenues generated from collecting civil penalty fines to execute any enforcement functions 341 
required by this act.  342
