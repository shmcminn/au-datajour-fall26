Secretary Howard: 
 
Today, along with Councilmembers Bonds, Lewis George, Nadeau, Pinto, and Robert White, I am 
introducing the “Youth Advisory Council on Climate Change and Environmental Conservation 
Establishment Act of 2025”. Please find enclosed a signed copy of the legislation. 
 
Young people are some of the most passionate and informed advocates for climate action. Youth-
led movements—like the Fridays for Future campaign started by Greta Thunberg—have 
transformed public discourse while pushing for urgent policy changes to combat climate change. 
Youth voices have also taken center stage in forums like the United Nations’s Framework 
Convention on Climate Change Conference of the Parties (COP). Youth-led climate movements and 
action campaigns have been steadily growing over the last decade, bringing much-needed attention 
to attention to the urgency of the climate crisis. 
 
Despite these significant contributions, youth voices remain underrepresented in formal decision-
making spaces, including those in the District. Establishing a Youth Advisory Council dedicated to 
climate change and environmental conservation is a simple step to empower those most acutely 
impacted by the long-term effects of climate change. This bill recognizes the value of 
intergenerational collaboration and ensures that the perspectives of those most invested in the 
future are not only heard, but actively integrated into our climate strategies. 
 
During the Committee on Transportation and the Environment’s performance oversight hearings in 
early 2024, youth advocates made clear that they want to participate in climate change governance 
here in the District and engage more meaningfully with the Council, the Executive, and other 
stakeholders to ensure that climate action policies are inclusive and reflect the needs of 
marginalized communities. This legislation will achieve that goal, and I look forward to working with

my colleagues to pass it so that young environmental activists have a seat at the table and know 
that their voices are heard. 
 
Sincerely,

Sec. 2. Youth Advisory Council on Climate Change and Environmental Conservation. 35 
(a) There is established the Youth Advisory Council on Climate Change and 36 
Environmental Conservation (“Youth Climate Council”), whose mission is to: 37 
(1) Consult with and advise the Mayor, the Council of the District of Columbia, 38 
including the committees with jurisdiction over the environment, natural resources, and energy, 39

the Department of Energy and Environment, the Commission on Climate Change and Resiliency, 40 
and other relevant District agencies; 41 
 (2) Comment on legislation, laws, policies, and practices that impact youth and the 42 
environment; 43 
 (3) Identify climate issues that disproportionately affect youth and provide 44 
recommendations on how to mitigate and adapt to these issues and present methods to combat 45 
climate change; 46 
 (4) Monitor climate programs and policies that affect youth and ensure they are 47 
achieving the intended outcomes, including making recommendations on environmental education 48 
and outdoor recreation programs in schools and those directed at youth and their families; and  49 
 (5) Collaborate with other youth organizations and entities in the District, including 50 
the Youth Advisory Council (D.C. Official Code § 2–1562). 51 
(b) The Youth Climate Council shall have 17 voting members, who shall be appointed as 52 
follows: 53 
(1) Nine members appointed by the Mayor for a term of 3 years, with initial 54 
staggered appointments of 5 voting members appointed for 2 years and 4 voting members 55 
appointed for 1 year, and shall be: 56 
   (A) Three middle school students;  57 
 (B) Three high school students; and 58 
 (C) Three college or university students from District-area colleges and 59 
universities. 60

Sec. 3. Functions and procedures of the Youth Climate Council. 90 
(a)(1) The Youth Climate Council shall elect a chairperson, vice-chairperson, and secretary 91 
for a term of one year.  92 
(2) These officers may be re-elected but shall serve no more than two (2) terms in 93 
any combination of offices.  94 
(3) The Youth Climate Council may vote to create other leadership positions from 95 
within its membership.  96 
(b) The Youth Climate Council shall meet at least once per quarter. 97 
(c) The chairperson of the Commission, or vice-chairperson in their absence, shall convene 98 
all Youth Climate Council meetings. 99 
(d)(1) The first Youth Climate Council shall create bylaws and rules to which all future 100 
Youth Climate Councils shall adhere.  101 
(2) Where not otherwise provided in this act or the bylaws and rules, the procedures of 102 
the Youth Climate Council shall be governed by Robert’s Rules of Order. 103 
(d) A majority of the voting members appointed to the Youth Climate Council at any given 104 
time shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of official business. 105

(e) All actions of the Youth Climate Council shall be considered advisory and shall be 106 
taken by majority vote of the voting members present and voting at a meeting. 107 
(f) The Youth Climate Council may form committees or working groups from its 108 
membership to consider specific subtopics and invite participation from outside individuals and 109 
entities, including the public and subject-matter experts. 110 
(g) The Department of Energy and Environment shall provide principal staff and 111 
equipment support for the activities of the Youth Climate Council and assist with the development, 112 
publication, and submission of all reports to the Council and Mayor.  113 
(h) The Council may provide staff assistance to the Youth Climate Council for the purposes 114 
of assistance with comments on all proposed legislation pending before the Council.  115 
(i) The chairpersons of the committees with jurisdiction over the environment, energy and 116 
natural resources at the Council shall provide an opportunity for the Youth Climate Council to 117 
report in person on its findings and recommendations at least once each year. 118 
(j) The Youth Climate Council may use space and supplies owned or rented by the District 119 
government for purposes consistent with this chapter. 120
