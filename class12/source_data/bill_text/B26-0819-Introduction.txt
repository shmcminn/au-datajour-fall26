To amend The Condominium Act of 1976 to require condominium unit owners associations to 12 
conduct remaining life, repair, and replacement cost studies, to amend the Inclusionary 13 
Zoning Implementation Amendment Act of 2006 to require the Department of Housing 14 
and Community Development to subsidize unrealized equity increases in an 15 
Inclusionary Zoning ownership unit sale, to amend the Home Purchase Assistance Fund 16 
Act of 1978 to allow for asynchronous group education Home Purchase Assistance 17 
Program sessions and to allow qualified lenders to underwrite and assess qualification of 18 
Home Purchase Assistance Program loans, to amend the District of Columbia Housing 19 
Finance Agency Act to create a local residential mortgage foreclosure prevention 20 
program, establish the guidelines of the program and the criteria of a qualified applicant, 21 
establish a fund for the program, and fund the program through fees on closing and 22 
foreclosure filings. 23 
BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, 24 
That this act may be cited as the “Ownership and Wealth-building for our Neighbors (OWN) 25 
Omnibus Amendment Act of 2026”  26 
 Sec. 2 The Condominium Act of 1976, effective March 29, 1977 (D.C. Law 1-89 D.C. 27 
Official Code § 42–1901 et seq.), is amended as follows: 28 
(a)Section 102 is amended as follows: 29 
 (1) A new subparagraph (27A) is added to read as follows: 30 
  “(27A) Reserve study” means a capital budget planning tool used to determine the 31 
estimated remaining useful life of, and estimated repair or replacement cost of common element 32 
components, including but not limited to common element structural components, or other 33 
components of the condominium, or other improvements for which the unit owners association is 34 
responsible for the repair and replacement,  and an analysis of association funding capacity to 35

maintain, repair, and replace such components and recommendations for funding, and all condo 36 
fee increases within the past 5 years or the lifetime of the building, whichever is lesser. 37 
 (b) A new section 307a is added to read as follows: 38 
 “(a) If the executive board oversees common elements with an aggregate replacement 39 
cost exceeding $10,000 and has had a reserve study conducted on or after January 1, 2023, then 40 
the executive board shall conduct an updated reserve study within 5 years after the date the 41 
reserve study was conducted, and at least every 5 years thereafter to assess the condition of and 42 
planning for repair and maintenance of the common elements and other components of the 43 
condominium for which the unit owners association is responsible. 44 
“(b) If the executive board oversees common elements with an aggregate replacement 45 
cost exceeding $10,000 and has not conducted a reserve study  on or after January 1, 2023, then 46 
the executive board shall conduct a reserve study  on or before January 1, 2028, and shall update 47 
the study at least every 5 years thereafter to assess the condition of and planning for repair and 48 
maintenance of the common elements and other components of the condominium for which the 49 
unit owners association is responsible. 50 
 “(c) Each reserve study required under this section shall be prepared by or attested to by a 51 
person who either: 52 
“(1) Holds a current license from the Board of Professional Engineer, or Board of 53 
Architecture, Interior Design, and Landscape Architecture; or 54 
“(2) Is currently designated as a reserve specialist by the Community Association 55 
Institute or as a professional reserve analyst by the Association of Professional Reserve Analysts. 56 
 “(d) As part of its periodic budget preparation process, the executive board shall review 57 
the reserve funding annually for adequacy and make any adjustments to the annual budget and 58

annual assessment the executive board deems necessary to maintain reserves, as appropriate. The 59 
executive board may meet repair and replacement needs through replacement reserves, 60 
assessments, or borrowed funds. 61 
“(e) If a reserve study conducted in accordance with this section indicates a need to 62 
budget for reserves, the unit owners’ association budget shall include: 63 
“(1) As of the beginning of the fiscal year for which the budget is prepared, the 64 
current amount of accumulated cash reserves set aside to repair, replace, or restore the common 65 
element components, including common element structural components, and other components 66 
under association responsibility and the amount of the expected contribution to the reserve fund 67 
for that fiscal year; 68 
  “(2) A statement describing the procedures used for estimation and accumulation 69 
of cash reserves pursuant to this section; and 70 
  “(3)A statement of the amount of reserves recommended in the study and the 71 
amount of current cash for replacement reserves. 72 
“(f) The study shall be presented to the prospective buyer at time of sale” 73 
 Sec. 3. Section 103 of the Inclusionary Zoning Implementation Amendment Act of 2006, 74 
effective March 14, 2007 (D.C. Law 16-275; D.C. Official Code § 6-1041.03), is amended as 75 
follows: 76 
 (a) Subsection (c) is amended by striking “Except as provided in subsection (d) the” and 77 
inserting the word “The” in its place. 78 
 (b) Subsection (d) is repealed. 79 
 (c) A new subsection (e) is added to read as follows 80

“(e)(1) Upon the second or subsequent sale of an inclusionary unit, whether directly to an 81 
eligible household or through a purchase by the Mayor, the seller shall receive an equity payment 82 
from the Mayor. 83 
“(2) The equity payment shall equal the purchase price paid by the seller 84 
multiplied by the amount by which the market appreciation percentage exceeds the regulated 85 
appreciation percentage. If the market appreciation percentage does not exceed the regulated 86 
appreciation percentage, no equity payment shall be due. 87 
“(3) In a direct sale to an eligible household, the household shall pay the regulated 88 
sales price and the Mayor shall deposit the equity payment with the settlement agent before 89 
closing. The equity payment shall be disbursed only if the sale closes. 90 
“(4) The equity payment shall not increase the price paid by the next eligible 91 
household, be financed by that household, or be included when calculating a future maximum 92 
purchase price. The affordability covenant shall remain in effect. 93 
“(5) The Mayor, pursuant to § 2-501 et seq., may issue rules to implement the 94 
provisions of this subsection.  95 
“(6) For purposes of this subsection: 96 
“(A) ‘Market appreciation percentage’ means the percentage increase from 97 
the appraised market value of the inclusionary unit when the seller purchased it to the appraised 98 
market value when the seller sells it. Each appraisal shall determine the market value of the unit 99 
without regard to the affordability covenant. 100 
“(B) ‘Regulated appreciation percentage’ means: 101

“(i) If the maximum purchase price under subsection (c) of this 102 
section is calculated using the consumer price index, the percentage by which the consumer price 103 
index has increased since the seller purchased the unit; or 104 
“(ii) If the Mayor uses another formula under subsection (c) of this 105 
section, the percentage increase in the seller’s purchase price provided by that formula, excluding 106 
adjustments for eligible improvements. 107 
“(iii) The regulated appreciation percentage shall not be less than 108 
zero. 109

 Sec. 4. The Home Purchase Assistance Fund Act of 1978, effective September 12, 1978 111 
(D.C. Law 2-103; D.C. Official Code § 42-2601 et seq.), is amended as follows: 112 
(a) Section 3a(f)(1) (D.C. Official Code § 42-2602.01(f)(1)) is amended by striking the 113 
phrase "this section" and inserting the phrase "this section, section 3a-1, and section 3a-2" in its 114 
place. 115 
(b) New sections 3a-1 and 3a-2 are added to read as follows: 116 
"Sec. 3a-1. Qualification for Program assistance. 117 
"(a) An applicant shall apply for Program assistance through a participating lender.  118 
"(b) A participating lender shall perform qualification concurrently with its underwriting 119 
of the first trust mortgage for the subject transaction, utilizing a uniform worksheet prescribed by 120 
the Mayor. The participating lender shall submit the completed worksheet and all supporting 121 
documentation directly to the Mayor. 122 
"(c) The Mayor shall make the final determination of applicant eligibility. The Mayor 123 
shall directly verify each applicant's income eligibility and shall not delegate this verification. 124

The Mayor’s review of an application file shall be strictly limited to assessing statutory and 125 
regulatory eligibility, underlying funding-source requirements, quality control, and the accuracy 126 
and completeness of the uniform worksheet. The Mayor shall not repeat or duplicate the 127 
participating lender's underwriting unless: 128 
"(1) There is a discrepancy in the submitted file; 129 
"(2) There is an indication of fraud or misrepresentation; or 130 
"(3) An applicable funding-source requirement is not addressed by the uniform 131 
worksheet. 132 
"(d) If a submitted file is deficient, the Mayor shall identify all deficiencies in a single 133 
written notice. The Mayor shall issue a final eligibility determination within 15 business days 134 
after receiving a complete file. The Mayor may extend this 15-day period by up to 10 additional 135 
business days for any circumstance described in subsection (e) of this section, provided that the 136 
Mayor issues prior written notice to the applicant and the participating lender detailing the 137 
specific reason for the extension. 138 
"(e) A denial of qualification by a participating lender shall be issued in writing, shall 139 
specify the reasons for the denial, and shall not terminate the applicant's eligibility to seek 140 
Program assistance elsewhere. Documentation verified by a participating lender shall remain 141 
valid and transferable for 120 days for use by another participating lender, unless a fact material 142 
to the applicant's eligibility has changed. 143 
"(f) A participating lender shall not charge an applicant any fee for performing 144 
qualification, submitting an application to the Program, or receiving Program assistance. 145 
"(g) Not less than once annually, the Mayor shall review a representative sample of 146 
qualification files from each participating lender. The Mayor shall evaluate each participating 147

lender's qualification and denial rates for disparities based on any trait protected by the Human 148 
Rights Act of 1977, effective December 13, 1977 (D.C. Law 2-38; D.C. Official Code § 2-149 
1401.01 et seq.). The Mayor may mandate corrective action or suspend or terminate a 150 
participating lender agreement accordingly. 151 
"(h) Annually, the Mayor shall publish and transmit a report to the Council detailing: 152 
"(1) The number of participating lenders and the number of those lenders that 153 
qualified at least one applicant; 154 
"(2) The number of applicants qualified, denied, and closed by each participating 155 
lender; 156 
"(3) The median and 90th percentile number of business days elapsed from receipt 157 
of a complete file to the issuance of a determination; and 158 
"(4) The number of files returned for deficiencies. 159 
"(i) The Mayor shall disaggregate the data required by this subsection by race, sex, 160 
income band, and ward, provided that the applicant has voluntarily furnished this demographic 161 
information and its disclosure would not identify an individual applicant. 162 
"(j) For the purposes of this section, the term: 163 
"(1) 'Participating lender' means a lender that maintains an executed participating 164 
lender agreement with the Mayor. 165 
"(2) 'Qualification' means the verification of assets, liabilities, credit history, and 166 
repayment capacity; the terms of the proposed first trust mortgage; the appraised value of the 167 
property to be purchased; and the amount of Program assistance for which the applicant appears 168 
eligible. 169 
"Sec. 3a-2. Homebuyer education and counseling. 170

"(a) The Mayor shall make group homebuyer education available in a self-paced, 171 
asynchronous format at no charge to participants. Successful completion of this curriculum 172 
satisfies any group homebuyer education requirement established by the Program. This 173 
requirements shall not be applicable to individualized housing counseling that is required by 24 174 
C.F.R. § 92.254(a)(3). 175 
"(b) The asynchronous group homebuyer education platform shall: 176 
"(1) Operate compatibly on mobile devices; 177 
"(2) Automatically preserve and record a participant's progress; 178 
"(3) Comply with all applicable federal and District accessibility standards; 179 
"(c) The Mayor shall establish objective completion standards for asynchronous group 180 
homebuyer education that verify active participation and substantive comprehension of 181 
curriculum content. These standards shall allow participants to retake course assessments without 182 
penalty. 183 
"(d) The Mayor shall publish annually on its website and transmit to the Council a report 184 
specifying: 185 
"(1) The number of participants choosing each respective format of group 186 
homebuyer education; 187 
"(2) The median elapsed time from enrollment to completion and the overall 188 
noncompletion rate for each format; 189 
"(3) The median wait time, measured in days, from an applicant's initial request to 190 
the delivery of their first individualized housing counseling session; and 191 
"(4) The rate at which participants completing each format receive an eligibility 192 
determination and successfully close on a home purchase.". 193

"(e) For the purposes of this section, the term: 194 
"(1) 'Asynchronous' means an on-demand instructional delivery method 195 
permitting a participant to initiate, pause, resume, and complete coursework at times of the 196 
participant's choosing, without mandatory attendance at scheduled sessions. 197 
"(2) 'Individualized housing counseling' means one-on-one professional guidance 198 
delivered by a qualified housing counselor, consisting of client intake, an evaluation of the 199 
participant's financial profile and the affordability of the prospective home purchase, preparation 200 
of a customized written action plan, and documented reasonable follow-up efforts. 201 
Sec. 5 The District of Columbia Housing Finance Agency Act, effective March 3, 1979 202 
(D.C. Law 2-135; D.C. Official Code 42-2703.01 et seq.). is amended by adding new sections 203 
307b, 307c, and 307d to read as follows: 204 
(a) “Sec. 307b. Residential Mortgage Foreclosure Prevention Program.  205 
“(a)(l) The Agency shall establish a Residential Mortgage Foreclosure Prevention 206 
Program ("program") that allows qualified homeowners to apply for and receive financial 207 
assistance for payment of past due residential mortgage payments, property taxes, property 208 
insurance debts, condominium fees, and homeowner association fees that have put the qualified 209 
homeowner at risk of foreclosure of their primary residence. 210 
“(2) The Agency shall make financial assistance available to qualified 211 
homeowners with a demonstrated ability to pay their housing costs going forward. The types of 212 
assistance shall be subject to Agency underwriting and have the following criteria: 213 
“(A) A low-interest loan that does not collect interest or require payments 214 
for at least the first 2 years for applicants with an income of 100% AMI or greater, based on 215

household size in accordance with the Washington Metropolitan Statistical Area as set forth in 216 
the periodic calculation provided by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.    217 
“(B) A zero-interest loan that requires repayment at the time of sale or 218 
refinance of the subject property for qualified homeowners with an income less than 100% AMI 219 
based on household size in accordance with the Washington Metropolitan Statistical Area as set 220 
forth in the periodic calculation provided by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban 221 
Development. 222 
“(3) The Agency shall establish a standardized application process, requirements, 223 
and underwriting criteria for qualified homeowners in need of the program. 224 
“(4) The Agency shall set an appropriate repayment timeline based on the 225 
qualified homeowner’s repayment feasibility and ensure that all associated housing costs will not 226 
exceed 50% of the applicant's income. The repayment timeline shall undergo an annual 227 
recertification. 228 
“(b) The Agency shall record a lien on the subject property in the amount of the financial 229 
assistance provided to the qualified homeowner. The lien shall be subordinate to all other existing 230 
loans on the subject property.   231 
“(c) The applicant shall be required to attend annual housing counseling with a HUD 232 
certified housing counseling service as defined in section 214 of the Housing and Urban 233 
Development Code [24 U.S.C. § 214.3].  234 
“(d) Other than instances of a refinance, no qualified homeowner may receive more than 235 
$40,000 in lifetime assistance. 236 
“(e) All repayments of loans administered by the program shall be remitted to the fund 237 
established in section 307d.   238

“(f) For the purposes of this section, the term:  239 
“(1) “At risk of foreclosure” means:  240 
“(A) A mortgage lender has provided a homeowner with legal notice that 241 
the homeowner is in default on the terms of a residential mortgage on the home in which the 242 
homeowner lives for failure to pay; or  243 
“(B) A homeowner experiencing financial hardship that will not allow for 244 
the homeowner to make upcoming payments on a residential mortgage, property taxes, property 245 
insurance debts, condominium fees, and homeowner association fees. 246 
“(2) “Financial hardship” means a material reduction in income or material 247 
increase in living expenses such as job loss, reduction in income, or increased costs due to 248 
healthcare or the need to care for a family member, that has created or increased a risk of 249 
mortgage delinquency, mortgage default, foreclosure, or displacement for a homeowner. 250 
“(3) “Exhausted alternative financing” means: 251 
“(A) In the case of a residential mortgage, a District homeowner who has 252 
sought alternative means of loss mitigation, which is evidenced by: 253 
“(i) A referral to the Agency from a HUD certified foreclosure 254 
prevention housing counseling service as defined in section 214 of the Housing and Urban 255 
Development Code [24 U.S.C. § 214.3]. 256 
“(ii) A Loss Mitigation Denial Notice or Denial Letter from the 257 
applicant’s lender. 258 
“(iii) A Loss Mitigation Offer Letter or Approval Notice from a 259 
lender that the applicant would exceed 50% of the applicant's gross income. 260

“(B) In the case of a non-mortgage default, a referral to the Agency a 261 
HUD certified foreclosure prevention housing counseling service as defined in section 214 of the 262 
Housing and Urban Development Code [24 U.S.C. § 214.3]. 263 
“(3) “Qualified homeowner” means a District homeowner who is experiencing 264 
financial hardship, is at risk of foreclosure, has exhausted alternative financing, and makes less 265 
than 250% AMI based on household size in accordance with the Washington Metropolitan 266 
Statistical Area as set forth in the periodic calculation provided by the U.S. Department of 267 
Housing and Urban Development. 268 
 "(4) "Subject property" means the home in which a homeowner who is at 269 
risk of foreclosure lives. 270 
Sec. 307c. Funding Foreclosure Prevention. 271 
“(a)(1)(A) For each residential mortgage loan originated within or outside of the District 272 
of Columbia and related to property located within the District of Columbia, excepting only 273 
reverse mortgage loans issued to seniors over the age of 62, the escrow agent or other settlement 274 
or closing agent shall collect and remit to the Agency a foreclosure prevention fee of $100 at the 275 
time of closing to be deposited in the fund established in section 307d. This fee may be financed 276 
in the loan and paid from the loan proceeds or from any borrower cash contribution at the time of 277 
closing.  278 
          “(B) At or before the time that the foreclosure prevention fee is assessed under 279 
subparagraph (A) of this paragraph, the escrow agent or other settlement or closing agent shall 280 
provide the borrower with a notice of the foreclosure prevention fee and its purpose. The Agency 281 
shall create a notice form that an escrow agent or other settlement or closing agent may use to 282 
satisfy this notice requirement.  283

“(2) For each residential real property for which a notice of default has been issued, the 284 
beneficiary issuing the notice of default shall remit a default fee of $250 to the Agency to the 285 
fund established in section 307d. The $250 payment shall be required per property and not per 286 
notice of default. The beneficiary shall not, directly or indirectly, charge the borrower for this fee 287 
at any time during the foreclosure process. 288 
“(3) The Agency may make policies and procedures related to the implementation, 289 
collection, remittance, and management of the fees and may enter into individualized agreements 290 
with third parties governing the efficient remittance of the fees. 291 
“(4) The obligation to remit fees required pursuant to this section shall survive any error, 292 
omission, or failure by a settlement agent, escrow agent, or other third party acting on behalf of a 293 
lender, beneficiary, or mortgage servicer.  294 
 “(5) A borrower shall not be held liable for the failure of another person or entity to 295 
collect or remit amounts required pursuant to this section. 296 
“(6) For the purposes of this section, "residential real property" includes residential real 297 
property with up to four dwelling units, whether or not the property or any part thereof is owner 298 
occupied. 299 
 Sec. 307d. Residential Mortgage Foreclosure Prevention Program Fund. 300 
 “(a)(1) There is established as a non-lapsing Residential Mortgage Foreclosure 301 
Prevention Program Fund (“Fund”), which the Agency shall administer in accordance with this 302 
section. The Fund shall consist of: 303 
“(A) Fees collected pursuant to section 307c; 304 
“(B) Appropriations made by the Council; 305

“(C) Grants, gifts, donations, and bequests accepted by the Agency for the 306 
purposes of the Fund; and 307 
“(D) Interest earned on monies deposited into the fund. 308 
“(2) Monies deposited into the fund, and interest earned thereon, shall not revert to the 309 
unrestricted fund balance of the District of Columbia General Fund at the end of a fiscal year, or 310 
at any other time, but shall be continually available to the Agency without fiscal year limitation, 311 
subject to authorization by Congress. 312 
“(3) The Agency shall use monies in the fund for the purposes of carrying out the 313 
Foreclosure Prevention Program established pursuant to section 307b, including:  314 
 “(A) Disbursing low-interest loans that does not collect interest or require 315 
payments for the first 2 years for applicants with an income of 100% AMI or greater, based on 316 
household size in accordance with the Washington Metropolitan Statistical Area as set forth in 317 
the periodic calculation provided by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.  318 
 “(B) Disbursing zero-interest loans that requires repayment at the time of sale or 319 
refinance of the subject property for qualified homeowners with an income less than 100% AMI 320 
based on household size in accordance with the Washington Metropolitan Statistical Area as set 321 
forth in the periodic calculation provided by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban 322 
Development; and 323 
 “(C) Funding administrative expenses associated with implementation and 324 
operation of the program.  325 
“(4) The Chief Financial Officer shall invest monies in the fund in accordance 326 
with § 47-351.02, and all interest earned from such investments shall be credited to the 327 
fund. 328

“(5) The Agency may issue policies, procedures, guidance, and forms necessary 329 
to administer the fund and may enter into agreements necessary for the collection, 330 
remittance, administration, and disbursement of monies deposited into the fund. 331 
“(6) The Agency shall include in its annual report information concerning 332 
“(A) Revenues deposited into the fund; 333 
“(B) Expenditures from the fund; 334 
“(C) The number of households served through the program; and 335 
“(D) Program outcomes and performance metrics established by the 336 
Agency. 337
