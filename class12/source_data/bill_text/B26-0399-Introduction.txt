To amend Chapter 23 of Title 16 of the District of Columbia Official Code to establish an 23 
additional permanency pathway that includes a circle of caring adults who provide 24 
support and legal relationships for young people ages 16 and older as they transition from 25 
foster care to adulthood.  26 
 27 
BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this 28 
act may be cited as the “Support, Opportunity, Unity, Legal Relationships (SOUL) Amendment 29 
Act of 2025”. 30

“Subchapter VII. SOUL Permanency Option. 54 
“§ 16-2399.21. Definitions. 55 
“For purposes of this subchapter, the term: 56 
“(1) “Caregiver” means an adult who is not the youth’s birth parent and who is 57 
committed to providing housing for the youth, meeting the basic needs of the youth, and holding 58 
rights and taking on responsibilities to care for the youth as specified in the support family 59 
agreement. 60

3      
“(2) “Parent and child relationship” is as defined in §16–2352(a-1) 61 
“(3) “SOUL” means a permanency plan for a youth in the care and custody of the Agency 62 
that allows the youth to establish a legal relationship with a support family, without terminating 63 
existing familial legal relationships. 64 
“(4) “Supporter” means an adult who is committed to holding rights and responsibilities 65 
to care for the youth as specified in the support family Agreement.  66 
“(5) Support family” means a legal relationship between a youth, a caregiver and either a 67 
caregiver or supporter. The support family may include additional caregivers and supporters. 68 
“(6) “Youth” means an individual who:  69 
“(A) Is sixteen years of age or older; 70 
“(B) Has been adjudicated to be neglected pursuant to § 16-2317; and 71 
“(C) Is in the care and custody of the Agency. 72 
“§ 16-2399.22. Selecting SOUL as a Permanency Plan. 73 
“(a) Unless the Family Court determines that changing the permanency plan is not in the 74 
best interests of the youth, the Family Court, after giving all parties the opportunity to be heard, 75 
shall change the permanency goal to SOUL if the youth requests that the Family Court change 76 
their permanency plan to SOUL by: 77 
“(1) Appearing in court and making the request on the record; 78 
“(2) Submitting a proposed support family form signed by the youth and their 79 
legal counsel; 80 
“(3) Executing an affidavit signed by both the youth and legal counsel stating the 81 
request; or 82 
“(4) Filing a motion to change the permanency plan. 83

4      
“(b) If the court determines that changing the permanency plan to SOUL is not in the best 84 
interests of the child, the court shall state its basis in writing. 85 
“§ 16-2399.23. Motion to Establish a Support Family. 86 
“(a) A support family proceeding shall be initiated by the filing of a motion to establish a 87 
support family for a youth with a pending neglect case under § 4–1321.01 et seq. 88 
“(b) A motion to establish a support family may only be filed by the youth, with the 89 
assistance of a legal counsel, as needed. 90 
“(c) A motion to establish a support family shall include: 91 
“(1) A description of the proposed support family, identifying each member of the 92 
support family by name and role; 93 
“(2) A statement explaining why the establishment of the proposed support family 94 
is in the youth’s best interest; 95 
“(3) Written consents, if any, to the proposed support family; and 96 
“(4) A proposed support family agreement that is signed by all parties and meets 97 
the requirements of §16-2399.25. 98 
“(d) Notwithstanding subsection (c)(4) of this section, the youth may file a motion to 99 
establish a support family if all parties do not sign the support family agreement. The motion 100 
shall identify the party who does not agree, the reasoning for disagreement, and the efforts to 101 
resolve the disagreement. 102 
“(e) The party who does not agree may file a praecipe or addendum alerting the court to 103 
the points of disagreement, the reasoning, and the efforts to resolve the disagreement. 104 
“(f) If the guardian ad litem does not support the support family agreement as being in the 105 
best interest of the youth, the court shall appoint a stated interest attorney to assist the youth with 106

5      
the support family proceeding. The guardian ad litem shall continue to represent the best interest 107 
of the youth. 108 
“16.2399.24. Allocation of Rights and Responsibilities. 109 
“(a) Unless the court specifies otherwise, one or more caregivers shall maintain physical 110 
custody of the youth until they are 18 years of age. 111 
“(b) The support family shall determine the allocation of the following legal custody 112 
rights amongst the members of the support family until the youth is 18 years of age: 113 
“(1) Access to information related to the youth’s education; 114 
“(2) The right to make educational decisions when a decision by a parent is 115 
required by law, regulation, or policy; 116 
“(3) Access to information related to the youth’s physical or mental health; 117 
“(4) Consent to health care when consent of a parent is required by law, 118 
regulation, or policy; 119 
“(5) Authorize release of information when consent of a parent is required by law, 120 
regulation, or policy; 121 
“(6) Make financial decisions when a decision by a parent is required by law, 122 
regulation, or policy; 123 
“(7) Consent to social and school activities of the child; and 124 
“(8) Consent to military enlistment. 125 
“(c) Rights specified in subsection (b) of this section that are not allocated to a member of 126 
the support family shall remain with the incumbent rights holder until the youth is 18 years of 127 
age. 128

6      
“(d) Notwithstanding subsection (b) of this section, the youth retains all existing legal 129 
rights or capacity to make education, medical, or financial decisions for themselves. 130 
“(e) The support family shall determine the allocation of the following responsibilities 131 
concerning the youth amongst the support family. 132 
“(1) Provide housing; 133 
 “(2) Provide food, clothing, transportation, and meet any other basic needs of the 134 
youth; 135 
“(3) Provide support for the youth’s education; 136 
“(4) Support the youth’s physical and mental health and well-being; 137 
“(5) Offer the youth guidance and consistent support through challenges; and 138 
“(6) Support the development of financial management, problem-solving, and 139 
independent living skills by the youth. 140 
“(f) If a support family has more than one caregiver, a lead caregiver shall be designated. 141 
If there is a disagreement concerning the rights and responsibilities concerning the youth or any 142 
other aspect related to the care of the youth, the lead caregiver shall make the final decision if the 143 
youth is less than 18 years of age. 144 
“§16.2399.25. Support family Agreement. 145 
“(a) The process for developing the proposed support family Agreement shall be youth-146 
driven, with support, guidance, and structure from the Agency. 147 
“(b) The proposed support family Agreement shall include: 148 
“(1) The allocation of rights and responsibilities among the support family; 149 
“(2) Designation of a lead caregiver, if there is more than one caregiver; 150

7      
“(3) A statement that the caregiver or lead caregiver, if there is more than 151 
caregiver, shall make the final decision if members of the support family are in disagreement 152 
concerning the rights and responsibilities listed in § 16-2399.24, or any other aspect related to 153 
the care of the youth under the age of 18; 154 
“(4) A summary of the financial responsibilities and expectations of the youth and 155 
the support family members, including how the subsidy funds may be distributed; 156 
“(5) A description of any resources or ongoing supports the Agency agrees to 157 
provide to support the support family; 158 
“(6) Information regarding Agency, District, state, federal or other public benefit 159 
programs that the youth may: 160 
“(A) Be eligible for if they remained committed to the legal custody of the 161 
Agency; and 162 
“(B) Retain eligibility for after closure of the neglect case. 163 
“(7) Details regarding how establishing the support family and the payment of the 164 
subsidy may impact the eligibility of the youth and their support family members for other public 165 
benefits; and 166 
“(8) Any other agreements or terms identified by the youth or support family 167 
members as being important to having shared expectations of how the support family shall work. 168 
“(c) The proposed support family agreement shall be signed by: 169 
“(1) The youth; 170 
“(2) All support family members acknowledging their agreement to the support 171 
family agreement including the allocation of rights and responsibilities with respect to the youth; 172

8      
“(3) The guardian ad litem, acknowledging that they have provided legal advice 173 
and counseling to the youth and the proposed support family agreement serves the youth’s best 174 
interest; and 175 
“(4) The Agency, acknowledging their agreement to the subsidy provisions and to 176 
any additional commitments named; and 177 
“(d) At any time between the filing of this motion and the issuance of a final order 178 
establishing the support family, the Family Court or any party to the support family agreement 179 
may request an amendment to the proposed support family agreement. 180 
“§ 16-2399.26. Parties to a Support Family Proceeding. 181 
“(a) The parties to a support family proceeding shall include: 182 
“(1) The youth; 183 
“(2) The youth’s parent, provided they retain legal rights; 184 
“(3) All proposed caregivers and supporters; 185 
“(4) The District of Columbia, including the Child and Family Services Agency 186 
and the Office of the Attorney General; and 187 
“(5) The guardian ad litem. 188 
“(b) The Agency may, upon request, provide support for a caregiver to access legal 189 
representation. 190 
“(c) The Family Court may, at its discretion, join other parties on its own motion or in 191 
response to a motion for joinder or intervention. 192 
“§ 16-2399.27. Notice. 193 
“(a) When a motion to establish a support family is filed, the Family Court shall promptly 194 
set a time for an adjudicatory hearing and shall cause notice thereof to be given to all parties. 195

9      
“(b) The Family Court shall direct the issuance to and personal service upon the youth’s 196 
parents of a summons and a copy of the motion to establish a support family. 197 
“(c) When it is appropriate to the proper disposition of the case, the Family Court may 198 
direct the service of a summons upon other persons. 199 
“(d) If personal service under this section cannot be effected, then notice shall be made 200 
constructively pursuant to rules of the Superior Court of the District of Columbia. 201 
“§ 16-2399.28. Conduct of Hearings. 202 
“(a) All hearings and proceedings conducted pursuant to this subchapter shall be held by 203 
a judge, without a jury. 204 
“(b) All hearings and proceedings conducted pursuant to this subchapter shall be recorded 205 
by appropriate means. 206 
“(c) Except in hearings to declare a person in contempt of Family Court, the general 207 
public shall be excluded from hearings and proceedings arising pursuant to this subchapter. Only 208 
persons necessary to such hearings and proceedings shall be admitted, but the Family Court may, 209 
pursuant to rules of the Superior Court of the District of Columbia, admit such other persons as 210 
having a proper interest in the case or the work of the Division on the condition that they refrain 211 
from divulging information identifying the child involved in the proceedings or members of their 212 
family. 213 
“(d) To enable participation by the youth and all members of the proposed support 214 
family, the Family Court and the Agency will work closely with these parties to schedule any 215 
hearings or proceedings. 216 
“§16-2399.29. Adjudicatory Hearings. 217

10      
“(a) An adjudicatory hearing on the motion to establish a support family shall be 218 
scheduled to occur within 90 days following the filing of the motion. 219 
“(b) The Family Court shall begin the adjudicatory hearing by determining whether all 220 
parties are present and whether proper notice of the hearing has been given. 221 
“(1) If a parent has been given proper notice but fails to appear, the Family Court 222 
may proceed in the parent’s absence. 223 
“(2) The Family Court may not proceed without the youth or any of the members 224 
of the proposed support family. 225 
“(c) Each party shall have the right to present evidence, to be heard on his or her own 226 
behalf, and to cross-examine witnesses called by another party. The Family Court may direct the 227 
parties to present specific evidence, including a background check of relevant background 228 
information.  229 
“(d) The moving party, the youth, shall have the burden of proving by a preponderance of 230 
the evidence that the proposed support family is in the youth’s best interest. 231 
“(e) In considering whether to grant the motion to establish the proposed support family, 232 
the Family Court shall take into account all relevant, material, and competent evidence presented 233 
at the adjudicatory hearing. Neither the spouse or domestic partner privilege nor the physician-234 
client or mental health professional-client privilege shall be a ground for excluding evidence. 235 
“(f) In its determination of whether to grant the motion to establish the proposed support 236 
family, the Family Court shall consider whether the proposed support family is in the youth’s 237 
best interest, based on the following factors: 238 
“(1) The youth’s need for a stable and permanent home, adequate care that meets 239 
their specific needs, and consistent and reliable caregivers; 240

11      
“(2) The physical, mental, and emotional health of all individuals involved to the 241 
degree that each affects the welfare of the youth, the decisive consideration being the physical, 242 
mental, and emotional needs of the youth; 243 
“(3) The quality of interaction and interrelationship of the youth with their 244 
parent(s), siblings, relatives, caregivers, and the proposed support family members; 245 
“(4) The terms of the proposed support family agreement, including whether: 246 
“(A) The allocation of rights and responsibilities amongst the members of 247 
the proposed support family is feasible and reasonable, considering the circumstances of the 248 
youth and all members of the support family; 249 
“(B) The fulfillment of the proposed support family agreement ensures the 250 
youth has a stable home and their basic needs are met; and 251 
“(C) The fulfillment of the proposed support family agreement promotes 252 
the youth’s well-being and growth toward independent adulthood; 253 
“(5) The youth’s opinion of their own best interests in the matter; and 254 
“(6) The impact on the youth of remaining in the care and custody of the Agency. 255 
“(g) The Family Court may modify the proposed support family agreement to more fully 256 
support the best interests of the youth if all parties to the support family agreement consent to the 257 
modifications. 258 
“(h) At any time between the filing of this motion and the issuance of a final order 259 
establishing the support family, the Family Court or any party to the support family agreement 260 
may request amendments to the proposed support family agreement. 261 
“(i) After considering all of the evidence presented, and after making a determination 262 
based upon a preponderance of the evidence that the proposed support family is in the youth’s 263

12      
best interest, the Family Court shall issue an order establishing the proposed support family and 264 
closing the neglect case, however the Family Court may not issue the order establishing the 265 
proposed support family until the following circumstances are met: 266 
“(1) The youth has been living in the proposed support family arrangement for at 267 
least 6 months; and 268 
“(2) The youth requests case closure. 269 
“(j) If the Family Court determines, upon a party’s request or at its own discretion, that 270 
additional time or evidence is necessary to determine whether the proposed support family is in 271 
the youth’s best interest, the Family Court may continue the adjudicatory hearing at a later date. 272 
“(k) A youth’s parent or permanent guardian as defined in §16-2382 may object to the 273 
transfer of any rights they hold under law and have those objections duly considered by the 274 
Family Court and addressed in written findings. 275 
“16-2399.30. Effect of a support family Order. 276 
“(a) A final order establishing a support family shall incorporate the entirety of the 277 
support family Agreement. 278 
“(b) The entry of a support family order closes the neglect case. 279 
“(c) The entry of a support family order does not terminate the parent and child 280 
relationship, including: 281 
“(1) The right of the child to inheritance; 282 
“(2) The parents’ right to consent to the child’s adoption prior to the child 283 
attaining the age of 18; 284 
“(3) The parents’ right to determine the child’s religious affiliation prior to the 285 
child attaining the age of 18; 286

13      
“(4) The parents’ right to visit or contact the child (except as limited by the 287 
Family Court) prior to the child attaining the age of 18; and 288 
“(5) The parents’ responsibility to provide financial, medical, and other support 289 
for the child.” 290 
“(d) Notwithstanding subsection (a) of this section, the government or the youth through 291 
their legal counsel may move to terminate the parent and child relationship, pursuant to § 16-292 
2354(a). 293 
“§16-2399.31. Jurisdiction. 294 
“(a) Subject to subsection (b) of this section, the Family Court shall have jurisdiction to 295 
enter a support family order and shall retain jurisdiction to modify a support family order until 296 
the youth reaches 21 years of age; provided, that when the youth reaches 18 years of age, the 297 
youth consents and the Family Court finds it is in the best interest of the youth. 298 
“(b) A youth who exits foster care to a support family may not reenter foster care after 299 
age 18. 300 
“16.2399.32. Support Family Order – Finality, Appeals, Transcripts. 301 
“(a) Every support family order shall be in writing and shall recite the findings upon 302 
which such order is based, including findings pertaining to the Family Court’s jurisdiction. 303 
“(b) Except as otherwise expressly provided by law, the entry of a support family order 304 
shall be final. 305 
“(c) In all appeals from decisions of the Family Court with respect to a support family 306 
order under this subchapter, the youth shall be identified only by initials in all transcripts, briefs, 307 
and other papers filed, and all necessary steps, as prescribed by rules of the District of Columbia 308 
Court of Appeals, shall be taken to protect the identity of the youth. 309

14      
“(d) Upon the filing of a motion and supporting affidavit stating that they are financially 310 
unable to purchase a transcript, a party who has filed notice of appeal or of interlocutory appeal 311 
of an order under this subchapter shall be furnished, at no cost or at such part of cost as they are 312 
able to pay, so much of the transcript as is necessary to prepare adequately and support the 313 
appeal. 314 
“(e) An appeal does not operate to stay the order, judgment, or decree appealed from, but 315 
whenever the case is properly before the appellate Family Court, that Family Court, on 316 
application and hearing, may order otherwise if suitable provision is made in the order for the 317 
care and custody of the youth. 318 
“§16-2399.33. Confidentiality of Records. 319 
“The provisions of §§ 16-2332 and 16-2333 shall apply to all records and files that are 320 
created pursuant to proceedings under this subchapter. 321 
“16-2399.34. Unlawful Disclosure. 322 
“Whoever willfully discloses, receives, makes use of, or knowingly permits the use of 323 
information concerning a youth or other person in violation of § 16-2393 shall be guilty of a 324 
misdemeanor and upon conviction thereof shall be fined not more than the amount set forth in § 325 
22-3571.01 or imprisoned for not more than 90 days, or both. A violation of this section shall be 326 
prosecuted by the Office of the Attorney General of the District of Columbia. 327 
“16-2399.35. Modification of a Support Family Order. 328 
“(a) The youth may move the Family Court to modify a support family order to: 329 
“(1) Add or remove members of the support family; 330 
“(2) Revise the allocation of rights and responsibilities; or 331 
“(3) Amend the terms of the support family Agreement. 332

15      
“(b) The youth’s parent or permanent guardian may move the Family Court to modify a 333 
support family order if the youth is under the age of 18 and there is a substantial and material 334 
change in the youth’s circumstances. 335 
“(c) Notice of a motion to modify a support family order shall be personally served on all 336 
parties, including any proposed additional members of the support family. If personal service 337 
under this section cannot be effected, then notice shall be made constructively pursuant to rules 338 
of the Superior Court of the District of Columbia. 339 
“(d) The Family Court may issue an order of reference directing the Mayor to file a report 340 
and recommendation regarding the proposed modification of the support family order within 45 341 
days of the filing date of the motion. 342 
“(e) A support family order may be modified if the Family Court finds, by a 343 
preponderance of the evidence, that it is in the youth’s best interests to modify the support family 344 
order. 345 
“(f) The Family Court shall hold an adjudicatory hearing before modifying a support 346 
family order and shall, at the conclusion of the hearing, enter a written order reciting the findings 347 
upon which such order is based, including findings pertaining to the Family Court’s jurisdiction. 348 
“§ 16-2399.37. Subsidy. 349 
“(a) The Mayor may make subsidy payments to members of the support family, 350 
irrespective of their state of residence, pursuant to this section. 351 
“(b) Members of the support family who may be eligible for subsidy payments under this 352 
section are limited to: 353 
“(1) The caregiver or caregivers who provide housing for the youth; and 354 
“(2) The youth. 355

16      
“(c) The Agency, the youth, and the caregivers must enter into a subsidy payment 356 
agreement that will be incorporated into the support family Agreement. 357 
“(d) Subsidy payments shall be allocated as follows: 358 
“(1) If the youth is 18 years and 6 months old or younger, the subsidy shall be 359 
paid to the caregiver or caregivers who are providing housing; 360 
“(2) If the youth is older than 18 years and 6 months, half of the subsidy shall be 361 
paid to the youth, and the remaining half shall be paid to the caregiver or caregivers who are 362 
providing housing. 363 
“(3) The Caregiver may choose to decline all or part of the subsidy, and that 364 
portion shall be paid directly to the youth. 365 
“(e) Notwithstanding subsection (d) of this section, the youth may at any time request the 366 
Agency to pay the entire subsidy to the youth. The Agency shall comply with this request if the 367 
youth is 18 years of age or older and can establish that they are not residing with a caregiver. 368 
“(f) Eligibility for subsidy payments under this section shall continue until the youth 369 
reaches 21 years of age. 370 
“(g) The Agency shall disseminate information to prospective members of the support 371 
family as to eligibility for subsidy under this section. 372 
“(h) Support family subsidies shall be subject to the availability of appropriations. 373 
Nothing in this section shall be construed to create an entitlement to a support family subsidy for 374 
any person. 375
