Statement of Introduction on the  
Transparency in Downcoding Amendment Act of 2026  
October 5, 2026 
Today I, along with Chairman Phil Mendelson, Councilmembers Charles Allen, Anita 
Bonds, Matthew Frumin, Janeese Lewis George, Brianne K. Nadeau, Zachary Parker, Brooke 
Pinto, and Trayon White, Sr., am introducing the Transparency in Downcoding Amendment Act 
of 2026. This legislation protects District of Columbia clinicians and patients from the growing 
practice of health insurers automatically downcoding medical claims and reducing payment for 
care that has been provided. Downcoding occurs when an insurer changes a service code submitted 
by a clinician to a lower-level code, resulting in lower reimbursement. Increasingly, insurers are 
using automated systems, algorithms, and artificial intelligence to identify and downcode claims, 
sometimes without a clinical professional reviewing the clinical record or the information 
submitted by the treating clinician. This practice can undermine clinical judgment, impose 
significant administrative burdens on medical practices, and unfairly penalize clinicians who care 
for patients with complex or chronic conditions. 
This legislation would prohibit insurers from automatically downcoding claims using 
artificial intelligence or other automated tools when the decision is based solely on a reported 
diagnosis code. It would also establish basic transparency and due-process protections: when an 
insurer downcodes a claim, the clinician must be clearly notified that the claim was downcoded, 
provided with the reason for the change, and given a clear and accessible process to appeal the 
decision. The bill would further prohibit insurers from using downcoding practices to target 
clinicians who routinely care for patients with complex or chronic conditions. 
This problem extends beyond the District; automatic downcoding has become a growing 
national concern. The American Medical Association has reported widespread use of payer 
downcoding programs, sharing examples of how insurers are increasingly using automated 
downcoding programs to reduce physician payments without adequate notice or review of the 
patient's medical record.1 Other states are acting: Illinois,2 Indiana,3 and Virginia,4 have enacted 
legislation prohibiting insurers from using an automated process, including artificial intelligence, 
as the sole basis for downcoding without clinical review, while also requiring notice, an appeals 
process, and protections against targeting clinicians who treat complex patients. In addition to

The District of Columbia should not wait for these practices to become more entrenched. 
Clinicians should be paid accurately for the care they provide, and insurers should not be permitted 
to substitute a diagnosis code reviewed by an algorithm in lieu of clinical judgment and 
documentation of the treating professional without a justification. This legislation will bring 
greater transparency, accountability, and fairness to health care reimbursement in the District while 
ensuring that patients with complex and chronic conditions are not indirectly disadvantaged by 
insurance payment practices. I encourage my colleagues to support this legislation.

To amend the Prompt Pay Act of 2002 to prohibit health insurers from automatically 37 
downcoding a claim using artificial intelligence and based solely on the reported 38 
diagnosis code; to require health insurers who downcode claims to notify the clinician to 39 
clearly indicate the claim had been downcoded; to provide clinicians with a clear and 40 
accessible process for appealing downcoded claims; to not target clinicians who routinely 41 
treat patients with complex or chronic conditions; and to make violations of this act 42 
enforceable by the Department.  43 
 44 
BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this 45 
Act may be cited as the “Transparency in Downcoding Amendment Act of 2026”. 46

Sec. 2. The Prompt Pay Act of 2002, effective July 23, 2002 (D.C. Law 14-176; D.C. 48 
Official Code § 31-3131 et seq.), is amended as follows: 49 
 (a) Section 2 (D.C. Official Code § 31-3131) is amended as follows:  50 
  (1) The existing paragraph (1) is redesignated as paragraph (1A). 51 
  (2) A new paragraph (1) is added to read as follows: 52 
“(1) “CARC” means the Claim Adjustment Reason Codes, which provide the 53 
reason for a financial adjustment specific to a particular claim or service referenced in the 54 
Accredited Standards Committee X12 835 standard transaction adopted by the Department of 55 
Health and Human Services under 45 CFR § 162.1602.”. 56 
(3) A new paragraph (3A) is added to read as follows: 57 
 “(3A) “Downcoding” means the unilateral alteration by a health insurer of the 58 
level of evaluation and management service code or other service code submitted on a claim, 59 
resulting in a lower payment to the provider.”. 60 
(4) Paragraph (5) is amended by striking the period and inserting the phrase “, 61 
including a third-party administrator or other payer responsible for adjudicating claims on behalf 62 
of a health insurer.”. 63 
(5) A new paragraph (9) is added to read as follows: 64 
“(9) “RARC” means the Remittance Advice Remark Codes, which provide 65 
supplemental information about a financial adjustment indicated by a CARC or information 66 
about remittance processing.”.  67 
 (b) A new section 5a is added to read as follows: 68 
 “Sec. 5a. Prohibition on downcoding. 69 
 “(a) A health insurer shall not: 70

“(1) Use an automated process, system, or tool to downcode a claim, including the 71 
use of artificial intelligence; 72 
“(2) Downcode a claim based solely on the reported diagnosis code; or 73 
“(3) Use downcoding practices in a targeted or discriminatory manner against a 74 
physician who routinely treat patients with complex or chronic conditions.”. 75 
 “(b) All downcoding decisions shall be made by a clinician licensed in the District and of 76 
the same specialty as the treating provider who shall perform a documented review of the clinical 77 
information supporting the billed service. 78 
 “(c) When a claim is downcoded, the health insurer shall notify the physician using the 79 
appropriate CARC and RARC to clearly indicate that the claim has been downcoded and 80 
provide: 81 
“(1) The specific reason for the downcoding, including by reference to the clinical 82 
criteria used to justify the downcoding;  83 
“(2) The original and revised service codes and payment amounts; 84 
“(3) The National Provider Identifier of the physician responsible for the 85 
downcoding decision, as well as the physician’s credentials, board certifications, and areas of 86 
specialty expertise and training; and 87 
“(4) A notice of the right to appeal as provided in subsection (d) of this section. 88 
“(d)(1) A health insurer shall provide providers with a clear and accessible process for 89 
appealing downcoded claims, including a written or electronic notice detailing how to initiate an 90 
appeal, contact information for the individual managing the appeal, reasonable timelines for 91 
submission of an appeal that are no less than 180 days, and timelines for the adjudication of the 92 
appeal. 93

“(2) A provider shall have the right to appeal in batches of similar claims 94 
involving substantially similar downcoding issues, without restriction. 95 
“(e) Any pattern or practice of discriminatory downcoding identified by the 96 
Commissioner or other regulatory authority shall be subject to enforcement actions, including 97 
fines, restitution, or suspension of health insurer licensure in the District. 98 
“(f) Violations of this section shall be enforceable by the Commissioner and may include: 99 
“(1) Monetary penalties of up to $50,000 per violation; and  100 
“(2) Orders to reprocess improperly downcoded claims with interest.”. 101
