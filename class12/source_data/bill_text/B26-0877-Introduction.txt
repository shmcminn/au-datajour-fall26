Statement of Introduction 
 
Contact Visitation Restoration Act of 2026 
 
October 27, 2026 
 
Today, alongside Councilmembers Brooke Pinto, Charles Allen, Wendell Felder, and Trayon 
White, Sr., I am introducing the Contact Visitation Restoration Act of 2026 to restore and create 
meaningful contact between incarcerated District residents and their families. The bill requires 
the Department of Corrections (DOC) to restore regular contact visitation at the Correctional 
Treatment Facility (CTF) and establish a phased pilot program at the Central Detention Facility 
(CDF), while maintaining appropriate safety measures. 
 
DOC suspended contact visitation at the CTF on May 15, 2025, without announcing a restoration 
date.1 Before the suspension, DOC facilitated 5,954 contact visits from October 2024 through 
May 15, 2025.2 Despite this established practice, the Department has not provided a clear 
timeline for restoring regular contact visitation. While DOC continues to offer non-contact 
visitation and limited family reunification opportunities, these cannot replace the ability to 
embrace a loved one. For children especially, the consequences of this separation extend far 
beyond the walls of the jail. 
 
The bill requires the DOC to restore regular contact visitation at the CTF within 120 days and 
launch a separate, phased pilot at the CDF within 240 days. The CDF pilot provides flexibility to 
address staffing, space, and security needs without delaying restoration at CTF. The legislation 
also establishes individualized, evidence-based standards for restricting visits, limits prolonged 
emergency suspensions, and requires accessible scheduling, family accommodations, and public 
reporting. These safeguards recognize that maintaining security and preserving family 
connections are not mutually exclusive. 
 
DOC has raised contraband concerns, but its reported recoveries occurred at both facilities, 
including the CDF, where contact visitation was never offered. Security requires targeted 
safeguards and prevention measures rather than blanket restrictions on contact with loved ones. 
Protecting public safety should not come at the expense of human dignity. The Contact 
Visitation Restoration Act understands that every day without contact visitation is another day 
families are unnecessarily separated, and we must act now to restore the dignity, connection, and 
humanity that no one should lose behind bars.

This legislation builds on my longstanding commitment to ensuring that incarceration does not 
erase a person's humanity, citizenship, or connection to family. In 2019, I introduced the Restore 
the Vote Amendment Act, which ultimately restored voting rights to District residents 
incarcerated for felony convictions. In 2020, I introduced the Helping Children Impacted by 
Parental Incarceration Amendment Act, requiring the District to assess the needs of children with 
incarcerated parents and develop a plan to support them. Both efforts reflected a fundamental 
belief that people in custody remain members of our community and that their children should 
not bear additional punishment because of a parent's incarceration. 
 
Before pursuing this legislation, Councilmember Brooke Pinto and I called on DOC to restore 
contact visitation voluntarily. But families should not have to wait indefinitely for the 
opportunity to hold their loved ones. This bill provides a clear, accountable path forward that 
respects DOC's operational responsibilities while recognizing the importance of family 
relationships to rehabilitation, successful reentry, and community well-being. 
 
This bill was drafted in collaboration with and supported by the DC Justice Lab, the Council for 
Court Excellence, the National Reentry Network for Returning Citizens, the ACLU-D.C., and the 
DC Fiscal Policy Institute.

38 Sec. 2. Definitions. 
39 For the purposes of this act, the term: 
40 (a) “Correctional Treatment Facility” or “CTF” means the Correctional Treatment Facility 
41 operated by or under the authority of the Department.

44 (c) “Contact visitation” means an in-person visit during which a resident and an approved

51 Sec. 3. Scope, eligibility, and access to contact visitation. 
 
52 (a) The Department shall provide each resident at CTF a meaningful opportunity to 
 
53 participate in regularly scheduled contact visitation with approved visitors, in accordance with 
 
54 section 4. 
 
55 (b) The Department shall provide contact visitation at CDF to residents participating in the 
 
56 phased pilot program established by section 5, consistent with the scope and implementation 
 
57 procedures determined by the Department. 
58 (c) For residents at CTF and residents included in an operating phase of the CDF pilot, 
59 contact visitation shall be the presumptive form of in-person social visitation unless the 
60 Department determines, based on an individualized assessment and specific articulable facts, that 
61 contact visitation for a particular resident or visitor presents a substantial and identifiable threat to 
62 safety, security, or the orderly operation of the facility that cannot be reasonably mitigated through 
 
63 less restrictive measures. 
 
64 (d) The Department shall not deny contact visitation solely because of the resident’s

88 Sec. 4. Mandatory restoration of contact visitation at the Correctional Treatment Facility. 
 
89 (a) No later than 120 days after the effective date of this act, the Department shall restore 
 
90 and thereafter maintain structured contact visitation at CTF for every resident eligible under 
 
91 section 3. Restoration at CTF shall not be limited to a pilot program or conditioned on 
 
92 implementation at CDF. 
 
93 (b) The Department shall offer CTF contact visitation on multiple days each week, 
 
94 including at least one evening or weekend period, and shall provide a meaningful opportunity for 
 
95 each eligible resident to schedule visits with approved visitors. 
 
96 (c) The Department shall maintain sufficient staff, visitation space, and reasonable security 
 
97 and contraband-prevention procedures to operate CTF contact visitation on a consistent basis. 
 
98 (d) No later than 30 days before the deadline in subsection (a) of this section, the 
 
99 Department shall publish the CTF visitation schedule, visitor procedures, and process for 
 
100 requesting and rescheduling a visit. 
 
101 Sec. 5. Phased contact-visitation pilot at the Central Detention Facility. 
 
102 (a) The Department shall establish and operate a phased pilot program for structured 
 
103 contact visitation at CDF. The pilot shall be separate from and shall not delay the restoration of 
104 contact visitation at CTF under section 4. 
105 (b) No later than 240 days after the effective date of this act, the Department shall begin 
106 the first phase of the CDF pilot. The Department shall determine the structure, initial scope, and 
107 method of implementation of the pilot based on safety, security, staffing, space, and operational 
108 considerations. Contact visitation shall be available to each eligible resident included in a phase of 
 
109 the pilot, subject to individualized restrictions imposed pursuant to section 6. 
 
110 (c) No later than 90 days after the first phase begins, the Department shall expand the pilot

111 to serve additional eligible residents through a method determined by the Department. 
 
112 (1) Within 60 days after the first phase begins, the Department shall evaluate the 
 
113 pilot to identify any safety, security, or operational concerns and determine whether adjustments 
 
114 are necessary. 
 
115 (2) If the evaluation identifies significant concerns that require additional time to 
 
116 resolve, the Department may delay expansion for up to 90 additional days. The Department shall 
 
117 provide the Council with a written explanation of the delay, the steps being taken to address the 
 
118 concerns, and the revised expansion date. 
 
119 (d) During each phase, the Department shall offer contact visitation on multiple days each 
 
120 week, including at least one evening or weekend period, and shall apply the visitor-approval, 
 
121 scheduling, notice, restriction, suspension, and accommodation requirements of this act to pilot 
 
122 visits. 
 
123 (e) The pilot shall operate for at least 24 months after the first contact visit under subsection 
 
124 (b) of this section. The Department shall maintain contact-visitation opportunities for residents 
 
125 participating in the pilot during the pilot and while the Department and Council consider further 
 
126 expansion, subject to sections 6 and 7. No later than 60 days before the conclusion of the initial 
127 24-month pilot period, the Department shall submit to the Council recommendations concerning 
128 continuation, modification, or further expansion of contact visitation at CDF. 
129 (f) No later than 15 months after the first contact visit under subsection (b) of this section, 
130 the Department shall transmit to the Council a brief evaluation of pilot participation, safety and 
131 security outcomes, operational challenges, and recommendations for further expansion.

137 Sec. 6. Individual restrictions and review. 
 
138 (a) This section applies to individual restrictions on contact visitation at CTF and within 
 
139 the CDF pilot. 
 
140 (b) A restriction on a resident’s contact visitation shall: 
 
141 (1) Be based on an individualized determination supported by specific facts; 
 
142 (2) Be no broader or longer than necessary to address the identified risk; 
 
143 (3) State in writing the reason for the restriction, its duration, and any conditions 
 
144 the resident or visitor may satisfy for restoration of contact visitation; and 
 
145 (4) Be reviewed at least every 14 days while the restriction remains in effect. 
 
146 (c) The Department shall give the affected resident and visitor written notice of a 
 
147 restriction. Either may contest the restriction by submitting information and requesting review by 
 
148 an official who did not make the initial decision. The reviewing official shall issue a written 
 
149 decision within 14 days after receiving the request. A resident or visitor may be assisted by counsel 
150 or an advocate of their choosing, consistent with reasonable facility procedures; nothing in this 
151 subsection requires the Department to appoint or pay for counsel. 
152 (d) The Department shall provide an expedited review process for restrictions based on 
153 mistaken identity, inaccurate information, or a visitor-specific issue that can be promptly resolved. 
154 (e) When a restriction prevents all contact visits for a resident, the Department shall provide 
 
155 a meaningful opportunity for non-contact in-person or video visitation during the restriction 
 
156 period. If a restriction concerns only a particular visitor or type of contact, the Department shall

7  
157 allow otherwise permissible contact visits with other approved visitors. 
 
158 (f) The Department shall not eliminate every form of social visitation for an individual 
 
159 resident solely as a routine disciplinary sanction. A temporary limitation needed to address an 
 
160 individualized, immediate safety risk shall be narrowly tailored; facility-wide emergencies shall 
 
161 be governed by section 7. 
 
162 (g) The Department shall make applicable restriction, grievance, and review procedures 
 
163 available to residents and approved visitors in plain language and accessible formats. A resident 
 
164 or visitor affected by a denial or restriction may use the Department’s grievance or administrative 
 
165 review process without limiting the review rights provided by this section. 
 
166 Sec. 7. Emergency suspension of contact visitation. 
 
167 (a) The Department may temporarily suspend contact visitation for all or part of CTF or 
 
168 the CDF pilot only when the Director determines in writing that an emergency creates an 
 
169 immediate and substantial threat to safety, security, public health, or facility operations and a 
 
170 narrower response is not reasonably available. 
 
171 (b) A suspension under this section shall expire after 72 hours unless the Director makes a 
 
172 renewed written determination, supported by current facts, that the emergency continues to require 
173 the suspension. 
174 (c) For a suspension lasting more than 72 hours, the Department shall provide written 
175 notice to the Council and post public notice stating, to the extent consistent with safety and 
176 security, the reason for the suspension, the affected facility or housing unit, the date it began, and 
177 the expected date of restoration. 
 
178 (d) During a suspension, the Department shall make reasonable efforts to provide video or 
 
179 non-contact visitation, notify affected residents and visitors of cancellations and rescheduling

8  
180 options, and restore contact visitation as soon as the emergency no longer requires suspension. 
 
181 Sec. 8. Family-centered visitation and accommodations. 
 
182 (a) The Department shall administer contact visitation to promote the maintenance of family 
 
183 and community ties and support successful reentry. 
 
184 (b) The Department shall provide reasonable accommodations for residents and visitors 
 
185 with disabilities and make reasonable efforts to accommodate older adults, visitors traveling 
 
186 substantial distances, and families with young children. 
 
187 (c) Areas used for visitation with children shall, to the extent practicable, include age- 
 
188 appropriate seating, books, games, or similar materials and permit appropriate parent-child 
 
189 interaction consistent with facility safety and security. 
 
190 (d) The Department shall establish procedures allowing a visitor to use an available 
 
191 restroom during a visit without unnecessarily terminating or shortening the visit. 
 
192 (e) The Department shall provide reasonable access to snacks, food, and beverages during 
 
193 contact visitation, including through vending machines or substantially equivalent means, 
 
194 consistent with reasonable security and contraband-control requirements. 
 
195 (f) The Department shall publish plain-language visitation rules, including prohibited 
196 conduct and contraband rules, and provide the rules to residents and visitors before a scheduled 
197 visit. 
198 (g) The Department shall make the rules required by subsection (f) of this section available 
199 online and in CTF and CDF in English, Amharic, Chinese, French, Korean, Spanish, and 
200 Vietnamese and in any other language required by the Language Access Act of 2004, effective 
 
201 June 19, 2004 (D.C. Law 15-167; D.C. Official Code § 2-1931 et seq.). The Department shall 
 
202 provide interpretation and accessible formats as required by law.

203 Sec. 9. Audit response and operational planning. 
 
204 (a) Within 60 days after issuance of any final audit, inspection, or formal oversight report 
 
205 concerning visitation operations, physical security, contraband controls, or facility conditions that 
 
206 materially affect the Department’s ability to provide contact visitation, the Department shall 
 
207 submit to the Council a written response identifying: 
 
208 (1) Each finding or recommendation the Department accepts, rejects, or proposes 
 
209 to modify; 
 
210 (2) Corrective action for each accepted or modified recommendation; 
 
211 (3) The responsible office or official and target completion date; and 
 
212 (4) Funding, staffing, procurement, or capital needs affecting implementation. 
 
213 (b) The Department shall not delay CTF restoration under section 4 or a phase of the CDF 
 
214 pilot under section 5 solely because an audit or review is pending when reasonable interim safety 
 
215 measures can address the relevant risk.

221 visits, completed contact visits, completed non-contact visits, and canceled visits; 
222 (2) The number of residents subject to an individual contact-visitation restriction
