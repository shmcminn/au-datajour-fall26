Pursuant to Rule 401(b)(1) of th e Rules of Organization and  Procedure for the 
Council, this is to request, on behalf of the District of Columbia Uniform Law 
Commission, that you introd uce the proposed “Uniform Assignment for Benefit of 
Creditors Act of 2026.”   
Under the uniform act, a finan cially distressed business, an assig nor, may transfer 
control of its assets to an assignee, who acts as a fid uciary for the busin ess’s creditors. 
The assignee may sell the bu siness’s assets and  distribute the p roceeds to the business’s 
creditors. These proceeds go toward s payment of the creditors’ claims against the 
assignor. The Act creates an alternative to other procedures available to the assignor for 
winding up its b usiness, and provid es benefits that are not available through a federal 
bankruptcy case or a state or federal law receiv ership. An assignment under this act 
generally will be more flexible, quicker, and less costly than the other alternatives. It is an 
improvement upon these alternatives in five significant ways: (1) it is debtor-initiated; (2) 
it does not require judicial supervision; (3) it p rovides assurances to cred itors by 
imposing fiduciary duties upon the assignee; (4) it is designed to maximize the value of 
the business’s assets for the benefit of all creditors; and (5) it encourag es cooperation 
between the distressed business and the creditors by aligning these parties’ goals. 
The u niform act wou ld rep lace a rudimentary 1964 Act of Congress with a 
detailed, state-o f-the-art uniform act, developed based  on many decad es of experience 
with these transactions.  In addition, the uniform act is compatible with the recently 
enacted Uniform Commercial Receivership Act, D.C. Law 25-269.

The uniform act was completed by the National Conference of Commissioners on 
Uniform State Laws only last year in 2025 and already has been enacted, thus far, in six 
states (Alabama, Arizona, Delaware, Iowa, Nebraska, and Utah) and introduced in three 
more states (Colorado, Oklahoma, and West Virginia).
